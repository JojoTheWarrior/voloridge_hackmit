## Creative direction

Monochrome, Geist, generous negative space. Large white typography on near-black.
Type appears as the camera follows the end of the sentence. Match that sentence
to the actual mission composer, then pull back to reveal the product. Cursor
paths use eased motion; clicks get one small expanding pulse. Fast cuts connect
the research stages, while the key finding gets time to read. Research time cuts
are explicit. Maps, plots and reports are genuine outputs from the selected run.

The 30-second style preview demonstrates the motion vocabulary using real
homepage and dataset-library screenshots. Its sample question is an unsubmitted
draft. It contains no research results, launch click or simulated agent execution.
It is a style proof, not the first 30 seconds of the eventual final edit.

## Narration scaffold

Target 225–255 spoken words after substituting the real story. Bracketed passages
are editorial slots, never material to record or display. No number goes into the
film before it is checked against the chosen mission's output.

0–9: “[A concrete question about the selected topic]? The answer could change
how [the actual intended user] decides [the specific decision].”

9–23: “This is Kingdom. Start with a question, choose the relevant public data,
and begin an investigation. Here, we're connecting [source one] with [source two]
to understand [the specific uncertainty].”

23–42: “Devin works through the research inside the mission. You can follow the
steps, inspect the evidence as it arrives, and steer the investigation. In this
run, it [actual operation], [actual comparison], and checks [actual alternative
explanation]. Each step helps establish what the data can really tell us.”

42–60: “The key finding: [one supported sentence]. [One number or concrete
comparison, with its scope.] That matters because [the actual decision it changes].”

60–88: “Open the explorer to examine the result yourself. [Name the first real
interaction and what it reveals.] [Name the second comparison.] Select an item
to see [the evidence actually available]. The finding becomes something you
can inspect and question.”

88–103: “The report brings the conclusion, evidence and limitations together.
Here, it supports [action or next validation]. It does not establish [one material
boundary]. That distinction is part of the result.”

103–113: “[One sentence connecting the completed supporting examples to the
same workflow, or one more useful observation from the hero.]”

113–120: “From a question to an investigation you can follow. Kingdom.
Ask. Investigate. Discover.”

## Capture plan when Tom's missions are ready

1. Read each selected mission's complete thread, report and actual explorer.
   Choose the hero based on understandable evidence and a working interaction.
2. Lock the final script to those outputs, including dates and sample scope.
3. Capture clean UI states with the existing Browser skill. Prefer a readable
   crop; preserve source imagery attribution and material caveats when shown.
4. Record three actual explorer interactions and their before/after states.
   Note target positions for cursor choreography. No fabricated controls.
5. Render scene clips at 1920×1080, 30 fps. Add intentional transitions, camera
   easing and cursor overlays. Never animate an unperformed research launch as
   though it ran. Label elapsed-time cuts or replays where relevant.
6. Produce narration from the locked script, or retain a caption-led cut. Tom
   is not required to record a voiceover for this pipeline to work. Voice selection
   is still open; the current preview has original synthesized sound design only.
7. Assemble exactly 120 seconds; inspect frames around every cut and interaction,
   listen through the audio, and verify the MP4's duration, codecs and dimensions.
